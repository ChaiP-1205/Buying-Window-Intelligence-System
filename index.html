Finding Pinnacle's Next Customers Before They Raise Their Hand

A working prototype that surfaces facilities in a live buying window — so SDRs stop guessing and AEs walk into every call with a relevant reason to be there.

1The Problem

"Our SDRs know the target list. What they don't know is which accounts are ready to hear from us this week."

In SaaS, GTM teams have it easy. Signals like funding rounds, hiring bursts, tech-stack changes, or SOC-2 renewals show up neatly inside Apollo, ZoomInfo, or Clay. A marketer can build a scored list in an afternoon.

Industrial reliability is different. The signals that tell you a refinery or chemical plant is actively looking for a product like Pinnacle's Newton don't live inside those tools. They live in places no enrichment vendor packages:

Government incident reports
CSB, OSHA, EPA — 40-100 page PDFs describing exactly what failed, why, and what the operator now needs to fix
Turnaround announcements
Trade press, LinkedIn posts, tender databases — signal an 18-month buying window opens
Earnings call transcripts
Reliability capex mentions, maintenance spend commentary from operators
Industry publications
Inspectioneering, Hydrocarbon Processing — bylines from reliability engineers signal expertise and hiring
Regulatory filings
10-K disclosures on reliability spend, insurance filings, PSM audit outcomes
Leadership hires
A new VP Reliability or Chief Reliability Officer triggers a tooling review within 6 months, 90% of the time

All of these are unstructured — long documents, prose, PDFs. Clay and Apollo cannot read them. That's the gap this project fills.

5What This Doesn't Do Yet — And What Comes Next

This is one signal source, one part of the picture. To turn this into a real GTM engine, three things need to happen next.

Find the humans to contact
Right now we know which facility to target. We don't yet have the reliability engineer's email or the plant manager's phone number. Standard tools like Apollo and ZoomInfo cover HQ executives well, but site-level engineering titles are patchy. Next step: build a custom contact-enrichment layer that combines Apollo with industry-specific sources — API/SMRP member directories, Inspectioneering author bylines, LinkedIn Sales Navigator scrapes filtered to the specific facility.
Add more signal sources
CSB catches only the biggest incidents. To spot every buying window, we need to fuse 5–8 signal sources: turnaround announcements from trade press, earnings call transcripts, 10-K reliability capex mentions, insurance renewal windows, leadership hires. Each source adds a few hours of engineering; together they become a signal moat competitors can't buy.
Personalize the outreach
Once we know who to contact and why they're a fit, the next layer is drafting the actual first-touch email. Each SDR message references the specific incident, the failure mode, and how Newton would have caught it. This is the layer that turns a scored account into a booked meeting — and it's a straightforward extension of the AI extraction pattern already built here.
Close the loop with closed-won
The scoring weights in this prototype are educated guesses. Once the engine has run for 3-6 months alongside Pinnacle's real pipeline, we can look at which signals actually predicted closed-won deals and recalibrate. The engine gets smarter every quarter without new engineering.
The end vision: Every Monday morning, every AE and SDR opens HubSpot to a personalized queue. Each queued account has the trigger event, the buying-window urgency, the recommended talk track, the right contacts, and a drafted first email — all generated overnight by the signal engine. That's what modern GTM engineering looks like. This demo is the first working stage of that vision.

6Honest Notes — What I'd Flag If This Were Real

  • Scoring weights are educated guesses. In production, calibrate against Pinnacle's actual won and lost deals.
  • Product-fit judgment is AI opinion. A Pinnacle reliability engineer should review the top-5 flagged accounts weekly to catch misjudgments.
  • Single signal source. One source is a demo. Real defensibility comes from fusing many.
  • Contact discovery is the missing piece. Knowing the facility is not the same as knowing the person to email. That's the next build.
  • No feedback loop yet. Production needs closed-loop attribution — did the flagged accounts actually convert?
